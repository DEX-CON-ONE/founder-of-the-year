3. Posts

Launch post for the feature (use only when you decide to publish it) I asked an AI-assisted process to go through my own records, interview me, check what I said against public pages and my own repositories, and write a profile. It's founder-prepared, not independent reporting and not an award, and it comes with a verification sheet that says exactly what rests on what. The short version: I want small businesses to have a technical partner they can trust, and this year I've been trying to be one. Founder-prepared with AI assistance. [V10, V11, V13]

Post 1: for startup founders. Twice this year a developer pulled out of a platform I'd committed to deliver on a fixed scope. I carried on. A contact from my conference days got in touch on LinkedIn; his family-run agency and I built it together, and I reviewed the work, with my software factory's help, before anything went live. The Link Network is planned to launch in December, and we're already planning the next projects. You know your industry; you shouldn't have to become a software engineer to build a company in it. You need someone technical who takes responsibility for the result. [V10, V11]

Post 2: for small-business owners. If you run a small business you've probably been quoted for a website or a system without being sure what you were paying for. At Miaigi you get a written blueprint before any bespoke build, and where your need is a common one the build starts from a ready-made kit. I'd rather you understood the job than simply trusted me. [V04, V05]

Post 3: for AI builders and peers. In my software factory, every job starts as a GitHub issue. Agents scout it, scope it, build it and check it, with a label at every stage. Four things are never done by an agent alone: moving money, speaking to a client, merging into a client's site, and publishing. A named person decides, and the decision is recorded. It built its first client site end to end this summer, and that site is live. If you work on approval layers for agents, I'd like to compare notes. [V14, V15, V17]

Post 6: the heating firm (only with the firm's written permission). A family heating firm came to Miaigi because, as the firm told me, its previous web provider wouldn't hand over its own website without a fee. My software factory built it a new one, now live on its own domain. That's the industry I'm trying to be the alternative to. [V17]

Post 4: one small client, three stages (only with the club's written permission). A martial-arts club came to Miaigi for a website. The site went live in December with an invite-only members' portal behind it. By the summer a made-to-order fightwear shop had joined it. Nobody sold them a platform on day one. That's how I'd like all of it to work. [V07]

Post 5: the motto. At the top of the knowledge base my agents work from there's one line: "Build the machine that sets us free." The machine is the factory. The freedom is meant for the heating engineer, the tattoo artist and the founder who'd rather run their business than wrestle with its technology. [V20, V13]
