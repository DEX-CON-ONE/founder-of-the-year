# Choosing the story and framing it

Do this once before writing, briefly, and again only if reporting undermines the angle. Keep it to a page in `private/narrative-options.md` and a few lines in the editorial brief.

## Candidate narratives

Write down three or four genuinely different stories the material could carry: a decision under pressure; a problem and the response; a mission and what it costs; a practitioner's path to founding; a contrarian view of a market. For each, one line on the reader it serves, the tension, the best supporting evidence (ledger IDs), what is missing, and a high/medium/low on evidence, reader interest and risk (overclaim, privacy, identification, single source).

Pick one, say why, and keep the runner-up for the bios or pitch. If the founder prefers a story the evidence does not yet carry, say what it would need rather than writing it anyway.

## What the article is saying

One reader, one central takeaway, one tension that makes it worth reading. Choose the strongest supported angle rather than giving every business and milestone equal time. The proposition explains why this person and this work are interesting to this reader; it is not the founder's positioning statement.

## Headline and standfirst

Offer two or three materially different headlines when the choice matters, recommend one, and say what it promises. The standfirst adds subject, work and stakes; it does not repeat the headline. Concrete words that name the person, the people served or the distinctive approach beat abstractions. "Founder of the Year" is never the headline. Do not imply awards, proven savings, market leadership or a launched product without evidence.

## Tone

Clear, assured business journalism with restrained personal detail; warmer than news, firmer than a press release. Use the founder's perspective without adopting accusations about unnamed competitors as fact. Treat adversity with care and no spectacle. Avoid both hype and a body so hedged that no story remains.

## Plan

Open on a supported moment, problem or decision. Arrange by cause, decision and consequence; career chronology only where it explains judgement. Explain each business by what it does for whom. Close on a grounded next test or forward look.

## Editorial brief (`public/editorial-brief.md`, optional)

A page: audience, angle, takeaway, chosen headline and standfirst with the rejected options, words to use and avoid, tone, structure, and the privacy and evidence boundaries that constrain the framing. It explains choices; it does not claim an outside editor approved them.
