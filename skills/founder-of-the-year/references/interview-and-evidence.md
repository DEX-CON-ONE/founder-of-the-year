# Evidence: the ledger, the audit and the one batch of questions

## Claim ledger (`private/claim-ledger.tsv`)

One row per claim the article might make, from the first session. A ledger shaped differently from this can stay as it is, provided every claim keeps an ID, a status and a treatment.

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Claim ID and wording | Trace the sentence, not just the topic |
| Source and location | URL, file and section, interview session and heading |
| Source date and access date | Expose stale or undated material |
| Source type | Interview, founder-authored, company-published, client-authored, internal note, independent record |
| Status | Corroborated, attributed, conflicting, unverified, future intention |
| What the source establishes | The narrow supported fact |
| Restriction | Public, anonymised, private; naming or quotation permission |
| Treatment | State plainly, attribute once, qualify, omit, ask |

Quotations are recorded separately with speaker, source, date and context. Cleaning filler is not permission to change meaning.

## What a source proves

A source proves only what it can establish. Code is not deployment, adoption or personal authorship. A plan is not a launched offer. A CV is the founder's account. A company's testimonial page is the company's voice. A repository date shows when work was active, not when it went live. A domain registration is not a build date. An inaccessible page proves nothing either way. When two sources conflict, keep both in the ledger and avoid the disputed wording in public until the founder settles it.

Check first the things an editor would check first: identities, roles and ownership; any award, current offer and its stage; deployments and customer results; figures (baseline, period, units, source); credentials.

## Audit the founder's own properties

Before anything else public: the public register (entity, officers, status) and every figure, superlative, count, price and guarantee on the founder's own sites, with its exact wording, URL and what substantiates it on the page. Put side by side anything two pages disagree on. Rank what a sceptical reader would test first. Give the founder a short fix list; the article cannot be stronger than the sites it points to, and the article itself uses none of those figures until they are substantiated.

## The one batch of questions (`private/open-questions.md`)

Every gap or conflict that affects a sentence goes in the queue as one neutral, answerable question with: why it matters (the sentence it affects), who can answer, the default you will use if no answer comes, and its status. Ask the founder once, at a natural break, most important first, never mid-story. Use a structured choice for a yes/no or either/or; plain conversation for a story. Carry on under the defaults so the work never waits on a reply. When an answer arrives, update the ledger and the sentence the same session.

## Completion states

The interview can be complete while a figure stays unresolved. Gathering can be complete while the founder still owes one anecdote. Deliver the strongest supported article available, name the specific gap in the verification sheet, and keep unused private notes out of the pack.
